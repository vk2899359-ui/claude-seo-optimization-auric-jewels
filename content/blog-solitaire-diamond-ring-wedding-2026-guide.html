The Four Pillars of Solitaire Diamond Selection

1. Cut — The Soul of Brilliance

Of all the quality factors, cut is the one that determines how magnificently a diamond will live in the light. An Excellent or Ideal cut grade means that each of the diamond's 57 or 58 facets has been angled with mathematical precision to return the maximum amount of light to your eye — resulting in the fire, scintillation, and brilliance that makes onlookers pause.

The most popular cuts for solitaire wedding rings in 2026 are:

  • Round Brilliant — the timeless standard, maximising light return, flattering on every hand shape
  • Oval — elongates the finger gracefully, delivers extraordinary brilliance, and has seen a surge in popularity among Gurugram brides this season
  • Emerald Cut — a step-cut stone with a distinctive hall-of-mirrors effect; chosen by brides who appreciate architectural elegance over overt sparkle
  • Pear — the teardrop silhouette, supremely romantic and exceptionally rare in well-proportioned stones

At Auric Jewels, every solitaire in our collection carries an IGI or GIA grading report — ensuring the cut grade you see on paper is the brilliance you experience in person.

2. Clarity — Understanding What Perfection Means

Natural diamonds form under extraordinary pressure over billions of years. The internal characteristics that result — called inclusions — are unique to each stone and do not diminish beauty when they fall below the visibility threshold to the naked eye.

For a solitaire ring, we typically advise stones graded VS2 or above on the GIA clarity scale. At this level, inclusions are invisible without magnification, allowing the diamond's natural brilliance to dominate. Stones graded VVS1, VVS2, or Flawless represent the pinnacle of diamond purity — chosen by connoisseurs who value the absolute.

The critical principle: always view the actual stone alongside its certificate. A grade on paper is a starting point; the diamond in your hand is the truth.

3. Colour — The Warmth Spectrum

Diamond colour is graded on a scale from D (completely colourless) to Z (light yellow). For a solitaire mounted in white gold or platinum, stones in the D–G range are ideal — their colourlessness allows the setting's luminosity to frame the diamond without any competing warmth.

For a yellow gold solitaire — a setting choice experiencing a remarkable revival this season — H–J colour diamonds can appear equally beautiful, as the warm metal tone balances any subtle warmth in the stone. This is not compromise; it is connoisseurship.

4. Carat — Size and Its Relationship to Value

Carat is a unit of weight (one carat equals 200 milligrams), not size — though weight and diameter are closely correlated. A well-cut one-carat round brilliant diamond measures approximately 6.5 mm across. The jump from 0.90 carats to 1.00 carat is significant both in price and in the psychological satisfaction of owning a one-carat stone.

For Gurugram's wedding season in 2026, the most commonly selected solitaires range from 0.70 carats to 1.50 carats — a range that offers extraordinary visual impact while representing a stone that will appreciate in stature as it accompanies its wearer through life's chapters.

Setting Choices That Define a Solitaire

The setting is the architecture that holds a diamond's brilliance in place. For a solitaire, three classic settings endure:

The Prong Setting — typically four or six slender claws of white gold, yellow gold, rose gold, or platinum. Prongs hold the diamond high, allowing light to enter from every angle and maximising brilliance. The six-prong Tiffany-style setting remains the global benchmark for solitaire design.

The Bezel Setting — a continuous band of precious metal encircles the diamond's girdle completely. This setting offers excellent protection for the stone — ideal for brides with active lives — and conveys a clean, architectural confidence that is distinctly modern.

The Cathedral Setting — the shoulders of the ring rise gracefully, like the arches of a cathedral, to meet the diamond at its base. This adds height and visual drama, making even a smaller stone appear regal and prominent.

The Certification Imperative: IGI and GIA

In 2026, purchasing an uncertified solitaire diamond is a decision no discerning buyer should make. The International Gemological Institute (IGI) and Gemological Institute of America (GIA) are the world's two most trusted grading authorities. Their certificates provide an independent, objective assessment of every quality parameter — cut, colour, clarity, carat weight, fluorescence, and measurements — giving you complete confidence in what you own.
