What Is Temple Jewellery?

Temple jewellery originated in the temples of South India during the Chola dynasty (9th-13th century). Artisans crafted gold ornaments for temple deities, and dancers who performed in temples wore replicas. The designs feature divine motifs — Lakshmi, Ganesha, peacocks, mango (paisley), and lotus — hand-embossed in gold. Traditional temple jewellery is made in 22K gold using the repousse technique, where designs are hammered from the reverse side of the metal sheet.

Weight vs Presence

Temple jewellery achieves visual grandeur with relatively less gold weight compared to heavily studded designs. The repousse technique creates bold, dramatic surfaces using thin gold sheets. This means a temple necklace that looks magnificently grand may weigh 40-50 grams, while a similar-looking diamond-studded necklace might use 60-80 grams of gold plus stone weight. For 2026 brides dealing with gold prices at ₹1,42,000 per 10 grams, this weight efficiency is a genuine advantage.

Types of Temple Jewellery for Weddings

Temple Choker (Addigai)

A broad, flat necklace that sits high on the chest, typically featuring Lakshmi or peacock motifs. Worn as the primary or first-layer necklace. Often paired with a longer necklace below.

Long Necklace (Haaram)

A waist-length necklace with temple motifs, coin patterns, or mango designs. Traditionally the centrepiece of South Indian bridal jewellery, now adopted by brides across India for its dramatic effect.

Temple Jhumkas

Bell-shaped earrings with deity motifs, coin dangles, and granulation work. Temple jhumkas in 2026 range from delicate 8-gram daily wear versions to elaborate 25-gram bridal pieces.

Temple Bangles

Gold bangles with temple motifs embossed on broad surfaces. Typically worn in pairs or sets of four. The coin-edge design is particularly popular in 2026.

Vanki (Armlet)

An upper-arm ornament shaped like a serpent or V-shape, worn above the bicep. Once exclusive to South Indian brides, the vanki is now worn by brides across communities for its striking visual impact.

Kamarbandh (Waist Belt)

A broad gold waist belt with temple motifs, worn over the saree or lehenga. One of the most photographed pieces in 2026 weddings.
