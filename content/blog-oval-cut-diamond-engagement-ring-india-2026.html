Why Oval Diamonds Are Dominating 2026

The "Bigger Look" Effect

An oval diamond appears 10-15% larger than a round diamond of the same carat weight. The elongated shape distributes weight across a greater surface area, creating more visual presence on the finger. A 0.80 ct oval looks comparable to a 1.00 ct round — and costs 20-30% less. For value-conscious buyers in Gurgaon, this size advantage is irresistible.

Finger-Flattering Shape

The oval's elongated silhouette creates the optical illusion of longer, slimmer fingers. It is universally flattering across all hand shapes and sizes. This is one reason stylists and jewellery consultants consistently recommend ovals for engagement rings.

Modern Yet Timeless

The oval cut feels distinctly modern compared to the traditional round brilliant, yet it lacks the trendiness of shapes like marquise or heart that may feel dated in a decade. An oval diamond engagement ring from 2026 will look as relevant in 2036 and 2046 as it does today.

Choosing the Perfect Oval Diamond

Length-to-Width Ratio

The ideal oval diamond has a length-to-width ratio between 1.35 and 1.50. Ratios below 1.30 look nearly round. Ratios above 1.55 look overly elongated. The sweet spot — 1.40 to 1.45 — creates the classic, balanced oval shape that photographs beautifully.

The Bow-Tie Effect

Every oval diamond has some degree of "bow-tie" — a dark shadow across the centre caused by light leakage. A well-cut oval minimises this to a faint, barely noticeable shadow. A poorly cut oval has a prominent dark band across the middle that ruins the stone's brilliance. This is why buying an oval diamond online (without seeing it) is risky. At Auric Jewels, we show you every oval diamond in person and under different lighting conditions so you can assess the bow-tie before purchasing.

Colour Considerations

Oval diamonds tend to show colour more than round brilliants, particularly at the pointed ends. For oval engagement rings, we recommend G-H colour in white gold/platinum settings and I-J colour in yellow or rose gold settings. Going below J colour may result in visible warmth at the tips.

Clarity for Ovals

The oval's large, flat table means inclusions can be more visible than in a round brilliant. VS2 is the safest bet for eye-clean ovals. SI1 can work but requires careful inspection — the inclusion position matters significantly in oval cuts.

Best Settings for Oval Engagement Rings

Solitaire Setting

A classic 4-prong or 6-prong solitaire lets the oval diamond speak for itself. The clean band draws all attention to the stone. This is the timeless choice that never dates. Available at Auric Jewels in 18K white, yellow, and rose gold, and platinum.

Halo Setting

A halo of small round diamonds surrounding the oval centre stone adds visual size and extra sparkle. A 0.70 ct oval in a halo setting looks equivalent to a 1.00 ct solitaire. This is the value-maximising setting choice.

Three-Stone Setting

An oval centre flanked by two smaller oval, round, or pear-shaped diamonds creates a dramatic, elegant look. Popular for anniversary rings and milestone celebrations.

East-West Setting

Setting the oval horizontally across the finger — the "east-west" orientation — is a bold, contemporary choice gaining momentum in 2026. It is unexpected, stylish, and stands out from every other engagement ring in the room.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an oval diamond cheaper than a round diamond?

Yes, typically 15-25% less for the same carat weight and quality. This is because oval cutting retains more of the rough diamond, resulting in less waste and lower cost. At Auric Jewels, this savings is passed directly to the buyer.

Does an oval diamond sparkle as much as a round?

A well-cut oval has excellent brilliance and fire, though slightly different from a round brilliant. The oval creates broader flashes of light rather than the pin-point sparkle of a round. Both are beautiful — the choice is aesthetic preference.
