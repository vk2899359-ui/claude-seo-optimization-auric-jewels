What Is Polki Jewellery — And Why It Has Endured for Centuries

Polki is not merely a jewellery style; it is a centuries-old testament to the finest Indian craftsmanship. The word Polki refers to diamonds in their most natural state — flat, uncut, unpolished, and possessing an organic lustre that no faceting machine can replicate. Unlike modern brilliant-cut diamonds engineered for mathematical precision, a Polki stone carries the character of the earth itself.

Each Polki diamond is set by hand into a base of 24K gold foil — a technique called kundan setting — by master artisans trained in a tradition that traces its lineage to the Mughal courts of the 16th century. The foil backing creates a mirror beneath the stone, amplifying its natural glow and giving Polki jewellery that distinctive warmth and luminosity that photographs so beautifully and commands such presence in a room.

The result is jewellery that feels regal without effort — a necklace that commands attention from across a mandap, a maang tikka that captures light at dawn and dusk alike, a set that carries the full weight of heritage without ever weighing down the wearer.

In a market where shortcuts are common and quality varies enormously, this stage of the process is where the work of a master jeweller is distinguished from ordinary production.
