Polki vs Kundan: Understanding the Distinction

The two styles are frequently paired, and frequently confused. Understanding the difference allows a bride to make choices that are precisely right for her aesthetic and occasion.

Polki refers specifically to the diamonds — uncut stones, flat-backed, held in a gold foil setting (known as kundan work) that allows their natural form to be displayed. True Polki is Zambian or Indian origin uncut diamond, and its value lies in both the stone and the craftsmanship of the setting. Polki jewellery has a golden warmth because the back-foil and the 22K gold frame radiate their own warmth around the stone.

Kundan, strictly speaking, refers to the setting technique — a method of jewellery-making that uses highly refined gold (kundan) to set gemstones without the use of prongs or claws, creating a perfectly smooth, seamless surface. In popular usage, kundan jewellery may feature glass stones, semi-precious stones, or precious gemstones in this setting style.

The 2026 Polki Bridal Aesthetic: What Has Changed

The Polki of 2026 is not the same as the Polki sets your mother wore. The fundamental beauty remains — uncut diamonds, gold, handcrafted heritage — but contemporary bridal Polki has evolved in form, proportion, and philosophy.

Choker-first compositions. The choker necklace has emerged as the dominant Polki bridal statement in 2026. Brides are choosing richly detailed Polki chokers with layered pendant drops rather than the traditional rani haar formats. This creates a neck-framing composition that photographs beautifully and translates from the bridal ceremony to every post-wedding celebration.

Pastel stone accents. One of the freshest developments in Polki bridal jewellery this year is the integration of pastel gemstones — muted pink tourmalines, pale mint emeralds, soft blue sapphires — alongside the Polki diamonds. These colours move away from the traditional red-green meenakari palette, creating sets that pair magnificently with the pastel and ivory bridal lehengas that are defining 2026 wedding fashion.

Lighter silhouettes, deeper craftsmanship. The 2026 Polki bride is not interested in maximum weight; she is interested in maximum craftsmanship. Sets are being designed to be lighter on the body but denser in detail — more intricate wire work, more refined meenakari on reverse faces, more considered proportionality between the necklace, earrings, maang tikka, and haath phool.

Day-to-evening versatility. Many brides in 2026 are selecting Polki sets that can be styled in parts — wearing the choker alone for the post-wedding receptions, the full set only for the ceremony. This modular approach represents a new maturity in how Indian brides invest in bridal jewellery, prioritising long-term wearability alongside ceremonial grandeur.
