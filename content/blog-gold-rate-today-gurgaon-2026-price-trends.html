Understanding Gold Rates — 22K vs 24K Gold

Gold rates in India are quoted for two primary purities: 24K (pure gold) and 22K (jewellery-grade gold). Understanding the difference is fundamental to making an informed purchase.

24K Gold (999 Purity)

24K gold is 99.9 per cent pure gold with virtually no alloy metals. It has a deep, rich yellow colour and is the softest form of gold. Because of its softness, 24K gold is not suitable for most jewellery — it would bend, scratch, and deform too easily with regular wear. 24K gold is primarily used for gold coins, bars, and certain specialised jewellery pieces that are meant for collection rather than daily wear. The 24K gold rate is the benchmark rate from which all other gold purity rates are derived.

22K Gold (916 Purity)

22K gold contains 91.6 per cent pure gold and 8.4 per cent alloy metals (typically copper, silver, and zinc). These alloys add durability and strength, making 22K gold suitable for crafting jewellery that can withstand regular wear. 22K gold retains the warm, rich yellow colour that Indian buyers love while being strong enough for intricate designs. The vast majority of gold jewellery sold in India — bangles, chains, necklaces, earrings, and rings — is made in 22K gold.

18K Gold (750 Purity)

18K gold contains 75 per cent pure gold and 25 per cent alloy metals. It is harder and more durable than 22K gold, making it the preferred choice for diamond-studded jewellery where the metal needs to securely hold stones. 18K gold is available in yellow, white, and rose gold variants. The lower gold content means a slightly lighter colour compared to 22K, but greater versatility in design. Many contemporary bracelets, pendants, and diamond rings are crafted in 18K gold.

Factors That Affect Gold Prices in Gurgaon

Gold prices are determined by a complex interplay of global and domestic factors. Understanding these factors helps buyers anticipate price movements and time their purchases more effectively.

International Gold Prices

The global gold price, traded primarily on the London Bullion Market and COMEX (New York), is the primary driver of Indian gold rates. Gold is priced internationally in US dollars per troy ounce. When the international price rises, Indian gold rates follow — and vice versa. In 2026, international gold prices have been supported by continued central bank purchasing (particularly by China, India, and other emerging market central banks), ongoing geopolitical tensions, and the metal's role as a hedge against currency devaluation.

USD-INR Exchange Rate

Since gold is priced internationally in US dollars, the rupee-dollar exchange rate directly affects Indian gold prices. When the rupee weakens against the dollar, gold becomes more expensive in India even if the international dollar price remains unchanged. Conversely, a strengthening rupee can moderate domestic gold prices. In 2026, the rupee's trajectory against the dollar has been a significant factor in domestic gold pricing.

Import Duties and GST

India imports the vast majority of its gold. The import duty on gold — which has been revised multiple times in recent years — directly impacts domestic prices. Additionally, GST at 3 per cent is applied on gold jewellery purchases (on both gold value and making charges). Changes in import duty or GST rates can cause sudden shifts in domestic gold pricing.

Domestic Demand — Wedding and Festival Seasons

India is the world's second-largest consumer of gold, and domestic demand significantly influences pricing. The wedding season (October to February and April to June), Dhanteras, Diwali, Akshaya Tritiya, and regional festivals drive predictable demand surges. During these periods, premiums on physical gold may increase even if international prices remain stable, reflecting the heightened domestic demand.

Monsoon and Rural Demand

A good monsoon season boosts rural incomes across India, which in turn increases gold demand from rural and semi-urban buyers. Rural India accounts for a significant portion of India's total gold demand, primarily for jewellery. The monsoon's impact on gold demand is a uniquely Indian factor that global markets often underestimate.

Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Policies

The RBI's monetary policies, interest rate decisions, and gold reserve management can influence domestic gold prices. When interest rates are low, gold becomes more attractive as an investment since the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset decreases. The RBI has also been actively increasing its gold reserves in recent years, reflecting a broader central bank trend that supports gold prices.

BIS Hallmarking — Your Guarantee of Gold Purity

In a market where gold rates directly determine the value of your purchase, BIS hallmarking is the only independent guarantee that you are receiving the purity you are paying for.

What BIS Hallmarking Certifies

The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) hallmark certifies the exact gold purity of a jewellery piece. Since June 2021, BIS hallmarking with a Hallmark Unique Identification (HUID) number has been mandatory for all gold jewellery sold by registered jewellers in India. The HUID is a six-digit alphanumeric code unique to each piece, linking it to the assaying centre's test results.

How to Verify Your Gold's HUID

Every buyer should verify their gold jewellery's HUID after purchase. The verification can be done through the BIS website (bis.gov.in) or the BIS Care mobile application (available on Android and iOS). Enter the HUID number to see the gold purity, jeweller registration details, and assaying centre information. This independent verification takes less than a minute and provides complete assurance of your gold's certified purity.

Why BIS Hallmarking Affects Your Gold's Value

When you eventually exchange or sell gold jewellery, BIS hallmarked pieces are accepted at their declared purity without additional testing. Non-hallmarked gold may be subjected to XRF testing or fire assay, and the purity may be found to be lower than claimed — resulting in a lower valuation. BIS hallmarking protects both your initial purchase and your future exchange value. Making Charges — Understanding the Full Cost of Gold Jewellery

The gold rate is only one component of gold jewellery pricing. Making charges, which cover the cost of designing and crafting the piece, are an essential element of the total price.

How Making Charges Are Calculated

Making charges can be expressed in two ways: as a percentage of the gold value (common for most jewellery) or as a fixed amount per gram (common for plain gold pieces). The making charge reflects the complexity of the design, the skill of the artisan, the technology used (CAD design, casting, hand-finishing), and the time required for production.

How to Read Your Gold Jewellery Bill

A transparent gold jewellery bill should contain specific line items that allow you to verify the pricing independently. Here is what to look for.

Gold Weight (in grams): The net weight of gold in the piece, measured on a certified electronic scale in your presence.
Gold Rate (per gram): The prevailing rate for the gold purity (22K or 18K) on the date of purchase.
Gold Value: Gold weight multiplied by the gold rate.
Making Charges: Listed as a percentage or fixed amount, applied to the gold value or per gram.
Diamond/Stone Value (if applicable): Listed separately with individual stone details for certified diamonds.
GST (3%): Applied to the total of gold value plus making charges plus stone value.
Total Price: The sum of all components including GST.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between 22K and 24K gold rate?

24K gold is 99.9 per cent pure and commands the highest rate per gram. 22K gold is 91.6 per cent pure and its rate is proportionally lower — approximately 91.6 per cent of the 24K rate. For jewellery purchases, the 22K rate is the relevant rate since most Indian gold jewellery is made in 22K gold. 24K gold is used for coins and bars. At Auric Jewels, both 22K and 18K gold jewellery are available with BIS hallmarking.

Why does the gold rate change daily?

Gold rates are driven by international gold prices (traded 24/7 on global exchanges), the USD-INR exchange rate, and domestic demand factors. Since these inputs change constantly, gold rates are updated daily — and sometimes multiple times within a single day. The rate applicable to your purchase is the prevailing rate at the time of billing.

What are making charges and are they negotiable?

Making charges cover the cost of designing, crafting, and finishing the jewellery piece. At established jewellers like Auric Jewels, making charges are standardised and transparently disclosed.
