Understanding the Craft: What Makes Polki & Jadau Different

What Is Polki?

Polki refers to uncut, natural diamonds in their rough form — flat on one side and natural on the other — set in gold without further processing. Unlike brilliant-cut diamonds that are shaped and faceted to maximise brilliance, Polki diamonds retain the raw soul of the stone. Each piece is entirely unique. Each stone has a character that cannot be replicated by a machine.

The result is a jewel that glows rather than blazes — warm, ancient, and unmistakably regal.

What Is Jadau?

Jadau is the technique, not a stone. Originating in the royal workshops of Rajasthan and Gujarat, Jadau involves embedding gemstones — Polki diamonds, rubies, emeralds, spinels — directly into a gold base by hammering the metal around each stone without any adhesive or prong. This is an entirely hand-done process, and a single Jadau necklace can take master craftsmen weeks to complete.

The integration of stone and gold in Jadau jewellery is so seamless that the pieces appear to have grown organically, rather than been assembled. This is why true Jadau holds its value across generations, and why Jadau bridal sets in Gurgaon are increasingly sought by families who understand legacy.

How to Choose Your Polki Bridal Set — A Guide for the Discerning Bride

Match the Metal Tone to Your Skin

Polki's beauty lies in the warmth of its gold setting. Deep 22K yellow gold Polki reads magnificently against all Indian skin tones, but particularly against wheatish and deep complexions. Brides with lighter skin may prefer the slightly restrained warmth of rose-gold-finished Jadau settings.

Consider the Lehenga Architecture

A heavily embroidered bridal lehenga calls for a Polki choker rather than a cascading necklace — the two compete for visual territory. A lehenga with a clean, solid-colour bodice, on the other hand, becomes a canvas for a full Jadau layered set to take command.

Think in Complete Sets, Then Edit

Authentic Polki and Jadau sets are designed as coherent wholes. Begin with the necklace-earring-maang tikka combination, then decide which additional pieces — mathapatti, haathphool, nath — complete your specific look. Over-adorning in Polki is rarely the risk; under-adorning often is.

Verify the Craft — Not Just the Stone

The value of a Jadau piece is as much in the craftsmanship as in the material. Ask to examine the reverse of the setting — authentic Jadau reveals intricate gold work on the back of the piece, not a flat stamped surface.
