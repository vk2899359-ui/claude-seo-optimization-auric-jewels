BIS Hallmarking — Why It Is Non-Negotiable for Gold Investment

If you are buying gold jewellery as an investment, BIS hallmarking is the single most important quality assurance you should demand. Here is why.

What Is BIS Hallmarking?

The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) hallmarking system certifies the purity of gold jewellery sold in India. Since June 2021, BIS hallmarking with a Hallmark Unique Identification (HUID) number has been mandatory for all gold jewellery sold by registered jewellers. The HUID is a six-digit alphanumeric code that is unique to every piece and can be verified on the BIS website or through the BIS Care app.

How BIS Hallmarking Protects Your Investment

Without BIS hallmarking, there is no independent verification that a piece of gold jewellery actually contains the purity claimed by the seller. A 22K gold piece should contain 91.6 per cent pure gold — the hallmark guarantees this. When you eventually exchange or sell your gold, hallmarked pieces command full market value because the purity is certified. Non-hallmarked gold may be subjected to purity testing and discounted accordingly.

Making Charges Explained — Understanding the True Cost of Gold Jewellery

Making charges are the most misunderstood aspect of gold jewellery pricing in India. Understanding them is essential for any buyer who views jewellery as an investment.

What Are Making Charges?

Making charges cover the cost of designing, crafting, finishing, and quality-checking a piece of jewellery. They include the labour of skilled artisans, the technology used (CAD design, casting, setting), and the overhead costs of maintaining a jewellery workshop. Making charges are typically expressed as a percentage of the gold value or as a fixed amount per gram.

Typical Making Charge Ranges in 2026

Machine-made jewellery: 8% to 12% of gold value. These are mass-produced designs with consistent quality and lower labour input.
Handcrafted jewellery: 15% to 25% of gold value. These pieces involve skilled artisan work, intricate detailing, and often unique or limited-edition designs.
Lightweight and daily wear: Making charges may be higher as a percentage because the gold weight is lower but the craftsmanship required is similar.
Diamond-studded pieces: Making charges apply to the gold component; diamond value is calculated separately based on the 4Cs.

How Making Charges Affect Resale Value

When you exchange or sell gold jewellery, the making charges are generally not recoverable — you receive the prevailing market value for the gold content. This means that for pure investment purposes, lower making charges are preferred. However, for jewellery that you intend to wear and enjoy, the craftsmanship represented by making charges adds aesthetic and personal value that justifies the cost.

What is the difference between 22K and 24K gold for investment?

24K gold is 99.9 per cent pure and is used in coins and bars. It is too soft for jewellery. 22K gold is 91.6 per cent pure, with the remaining 8.4 per cent consisting of alloy metals that provide the strength needed for wearable jewellery. For investment in jewellery form, 22K gold offers the best combination of purity and durability.

Do making charges reduce the investment value of gold jewellery?

Making charges are not recoverable at resale — you receive the prevailing gold value for the gold content.
