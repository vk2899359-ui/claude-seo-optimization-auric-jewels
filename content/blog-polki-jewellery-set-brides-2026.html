What Is Polki Jewellery? The Craft Behind the Crown

Polki is the art of setting uncut, unpolished diamonds — as close to their natural state as possible — in 22-karat gold frameworks. Unlike modern brilliant-cut diamonds engineered for maximum light refraction, Polki diamonds are celebrated for their soul: the way they absorb candlelight, catch the warmth of marigold garlands, and glow with a muted, ancient luminescence that photographs cannot fully capture.

The technique originates from Mughal India, refined over centuries by master craftsmen known as Jadau karigars. Each piece requires weeks — sometimes months — of skilled handwork: setting each uncut diamond individually, layering with meenakari enamel on the reverse, and constructing frameworks of hollow gold that are simultaneously featherlight and architecturally grand. When you hold a Polki piece from Auric Jewels, you are holding something that cannot be replicated by a machine or replicated overnight.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Polki and Kundan jewellery?

Polki uses uncut, natural diamonds set in gold. Kundan uses glass or cut stones set in a gold foil base. Both traditions originate in Rajasthan and employ the Jadau technique, but Polki carries the additional intrinsic value of genuine diamond content, making it both more precious and a stronger long-term investment.
