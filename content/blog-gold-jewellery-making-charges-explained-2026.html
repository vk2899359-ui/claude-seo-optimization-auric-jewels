What Are Making Charges?

Making charges cover the cost of transforming raw gold into finished jewellery. This includes the artisan's labour, design, casting, polishing, quality checking, and wastage (gold lost during manufacturing). Making charges are added on top of the gold's intrinsic metal value and are quoted as either a flat fee per gram or a percentage of the total gold value.

What Drives Making Charges Higher

  • Design complexity: Handcrafted, intricate designs cost more than machine-made pieces
  • Brand premium: National chains charge 14-20% while independent jewellers charge 8-14%
  • Wastage: Some designs lose 3-8% gold during manufacturing, added to your cost
  • Stone setting: Diamond-set pieces require skilled artisans and careful setting work
  • Specialised techniques: Filigree, meenakari, temple jewellery techniques cost more

10 Strategies to Save on Making Charges

1. Buy Plain Designs for Investment

Plain gold bangles and simple chains have the lowest making charges (8-10%) because they require minimal craftsmanship. For investment purchases, prioritise simplicity.

2. Choose Machine-Made Over Handcrafted

Machine-made jewellery has making charges of 8-12%, while handcrafted pieces can reach 20-25%. If the design does not specifically require handwork, machine-made offers better investment value.

3. Buy During Festivals

Jewellers routinely waive or discount making charges during Akshaya Tritiya, Dhanteras, and Diwali. At Auric Jewels, making charge waivers of up to 50% are offered during major festivals.

4. Negotiate — Making Charges Are Not Fixed

Unlike gold rate, which is market-determined, making charges are set by the jeweller and are negotiable. Ask for a discount, especially on high-value purchases. Most jewellers will reduce making charges by 2-5% for purchases above ₹2,00,000.

5. Ask for Wastage Separately

Some jewellers bundle wastage (2-5%) into making charges, effectively doubling the cost. Ask for wastage to be itemised separately. At Auric Jewels, every bill itemises gold weight, making charges, wastage, and GST as separate line items.

6. Buy Gold Coins for Pure Investment

Gold coins have zero making charges. If your goal is purely financial — Akshaya Tritiya investment, wealth preservation — coins are the most efficient format.

7. Compare Percentage vs Per-Gram Pricing

At current gold prices, per-gram pricing often works out cheaper than percentage-based pricing. A ₹600/gram charge on 20 grams = ₹12,000. A 12% charge on ₹2,84,000 = ₹34,080. Same gold, three times the making charge.

8. Consider Lightweight Designs

Lightweight earrings and pendants (3-8g) have lower absolute making charges because the base gold value is lower. For daily wear pieces, lightweight designs deliver better cost-efficiency.

9. Exchange Old Gold

When exchanging old gold for new jewellery, you typically get full gold value for the old piece and only pay making charges on the new piece. This eliminates the need to "buy" the gold portion again.

10. Choose a Transparent Jeweller

The most important strategy: buy from a jeweller who provides fully itemised billing. If a jeweller gives you a single lump-sum price without breaking down gold, making, wastage, and GST separately — walk out. At Auric Jewels, transparency is non-negotiable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I lose making charges when I sell gold jewellery?

Yes. Making charges are never recovered on resale. When you sell gold jewellery, you receive only the prevailing gold value based on weight and purity. The making charges are permanently lost. This is why minimising making charges at purchase is critical.

What is a fair making charge in India?

For machine-made gold jewellery, 8-12% is fair. For handcrafted designs, 14-18% is reasonable. For highly intricate or specialised work (temple jewellery, filigree), up to 20-25% may be justified. Anything above 25% warrants questioning.

Are making charges the same for 22K and 18K gold?

Percentage-based making charges are the same rate, but the absolute amount is lower for 18K because the gold value per gram is lower. Per-gram or per-piece charges are typically the same regardless of purity.
