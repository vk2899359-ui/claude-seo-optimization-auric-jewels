How Gold Rates Are Set in India — The IBJA Benchmark

Before the data, a quick foundation that most buyers never learn until they are standing at the billing counter.

Gold rates in India are not set by your jeweller. They are set by the IBJA — India Bullion and Jewellers Association — which publishes an official rate twice daily based on international spot prices, converted to Indian rupees. This IBJA rate is the industry standard. Any reputable jewellery showroom in Gurgaon prices gold against this benchmark.

The formula for your final jewellery price:

Final Price = (IBJA Rate × Gold Weight) + Making Charges + 3% GST

Making charges vary by showroom and design — typically 8% to 22% of gold value. GST at 3% is fixed. The IBJA rate itself is non-negotiable — it is a market rate, not a showroom decision.

24K vs 22K vs 18K — Which Rate Matters for Jewellery Buyers?

This is a question most buyers get wrong, and it costs them in negotiations.

24K gold is pure gold — 99.9% purity. It is the benchmark rate you see quoted everywhere and is used for gold coins, bars, and investment products. It is too soft for everyday jewellery.

22K gold (91.6% purity, BIS hallmark 916) is the standard for Indian jewellery. Rings, necklaces, bangles, earrings — if you are buying traditional gold jewellery, this is what you are buying. The IBJA 22K rate is calculated as 24K × 0.9523.

18K gold (75% purity, BIS hallmark 750) is the preferred setting for diamond jewellery. The higher alloy content makes it harder and more suitable for holding diamond prongs securely. If you are buying a diamond ring, mangalsutra, or earrings, the gold component is almost certainly 18K.
